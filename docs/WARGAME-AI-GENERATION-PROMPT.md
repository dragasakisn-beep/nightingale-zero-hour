# NIGHTINGALE: ZERO HOUR — WARGAME AI SCENARIO GENERATION PROMPT

Create a complete, facilitator-controlled, **five-turn FULL MATRIX WARGAME** named “NIGHTINGALE: ZERO HOUR — Operation Broken Chain” for Keiser University course CIS4430 Contractual/Regulatory Compliance. This is a fictional, unclassified cyber incident and contractual/regulatory compliance exercise for 12 student participants divided into 4 teams (3 people each), plus 1 human Exercise Director/White Cell. It must be playable in one 180-minute classroom session.

GAME DESIGN / WORLD:
Contemporary 2026 fictional United States aerospace contractor. Technology: networked predictive-maintenance telemetry, cloud analytics, supplier APIs, enterprise IAM, logging and SOC monitoring. Cyber and information + economic/contractual pressure + supply-chain risk. Tone: technically plausible, high-stakes, professional, investigative, serious, not sensational. Victory condition: **cooperation** and organizational resilience; there is no student-run adversary team. A fictional attacker and compromised subcontractor are nonplayer threats controlled by scripted developments and the human Director. Game style: FULL WARGAME, not Quick Game. Five turns, approximately 20 minutes of primary deliberation each, embedded in a 180-minute event with brief, break, board hearing and AAR. Set in-game time across the final 72 hours preceding a contract decision.

ORGANIZATION:
Project Nightingale operates a fictional aerospace predictive-maintenance service. It processes simulated aircraft component telemetry and limited fictional defense-related records. A third-party software maintenance supplier has remote access. The system's regulatory applicability and contract terms must be established from the evidence and supplied fictional contractual language; do not presume every framework is a statute or every dataset qualifies as CUI. Some evidence indicates credential misuse and possible telemetry integrity manipulation. There is no demonstrated exfiltration at the start. A fictional $8 million opportunity creates executive pressure to make an unsupported readiness declaration. All people, contracts, data, aircraft and facilities must be fictional.

FOUR PLAYER TEAMS, KEEP EXACT NAMES AND ORDER:
1. **ALPHA — Cybersecurity Operations.** Public objective: triage alerts, preserve evidence, contain unauthorized access and recommend proportionate technical action. Private objective: independently verify the suspected supplier credential misuse before publicly asserting data theft; identify whether telemetry integrity has been affected. Starting intelligence: unusual account access and conflicting log data; no confirmed exfiltration. Roles: Captain/Incident Commander, SOC Analyst, Forensic Investigator.
2. **BRAVO — Legal, Contracts & Regulatory.** Public objective: identify applicable obligations, review supplier and prime/subcontract clauses, assess reporting triggers and ensure accurate external statements. Private objective: disclose that the supplier agreement's notification provisions are ambiguous and identify which terms require amendment; do not invent legal deadlines. Starting intelligence: supplier contract lacks explicit incident deadline and defense flowdown provisions are contested. Roles: Captain/General Counsel, Contract Specialist, Regulatory Analyst.
3. **CHARLIE — Audit, GRC & Assurance.** Public objective: establish audit scope, validate control implementation evidence, reconcile the SSP with actual access and create defensible findings and POA&Ms. Private objective: demonstrate that a legacy service account contradicts the documented MFA implementation; distinguish remediation from certification status. Starting intelligence: incomplete SSP remote-access description, aging control artifacts and open findings. Roles: Captain/Lead Auditor, Compliance Analyst, Remediation Manager.
4. **DELTA — Executive Leadership & Supply Chain.** Public objective: preserve business continuity, evaluate supplier dependency, determine risk acceptance boundaries and recommend an evidence-based board decision. Private objective: manage a critical supplier dependency and the simulated $8 million opportunity without directing false compliance representations. Starting intelligence: key maintenance component depends on the supplier; decision deadline is 72 hours. Roles: Captain/Program Director, Procurement Lead, Continuity Manager.

DO NOT create a separate Red team or player-run White team. The human Director is the White Cell and controls adversary behavior, external entities, injects and final decisions. Teams are allied but have conflicting constraints; each team is assigned exactly one captain. Do not invent Keycloak usernames or student identities. Roster remains unassigned until Director supplies real, enabled usernames.

GAME STRUCTURE: 5 TURNS, ONE PHASE PER TURN:
TURN 1 — DETECTION / T–72H: Supplier service account performs anomalous access; inconclusive telemetry integrity indicators. Deliver a shared SOC alert, Alpha technical log extract, Bravo contract scope note, Charlie SSP discrepancy and Delta supplier continuity note. Require evidence identification, preliminary classification, containment options and assumptions.
TURN 2 — DISRUPTION / T–48H: Aircraft maintenance advisory outputs conflict with engineering verification. Vendor opposes disabling access. Teams must negotiate containment, operational fallback, evidence preservation and vendor obligations.
TURN 3 — DISCLOSURE / T–24H: A fictional contracting representative requests a status update; legal reporting applicability is unresolved. Teams must determine confirmed facts versus hypotheses, review contract clauses, assign notification decision ownership and document gaps.
TURN 4 — ASSURANCE / T–12H: Independent audit discovers an SSP/MFA mismatch and additional findings. Leadership proposes placing all CMMC gaps on a universal POA&M. Teams must validate current requirement applicability, assessment eligibility, score and exclusions before making any status claim; build a credible corrective-action plan with owner, milestone and closure evidence. Never presume all gaps are eligible.
TURN 5 — BOARD DECISION / T–0H: A fictional government coordination cell identifies similar supplier activity elsewhere. Leadership must recommend whether and how to proceed with business commitments, with verified controls and uncertainties disclosed. Require a single integrated readiness recommendation supported by each team's evidence.

OPENING INTELLIGENCE: Create one public briefing containing the fictional company system overview, unverified incident summary, time-sensitive decision and evidence boundaries. Create at least one private opening packet PER TEAM and one additional scheduled or Director-controlled inject per team across the five turns. Each packet must clearly identify source, confidence, turn, authorized audience and what is fact, assumption or unverified claim. Maintain fog of information; never disclose the instructor's ground-truth notes to players.

INSTRUCTOR CONTROL AND CONSEQUENCES:
Use the matrix move lifecycle: captain proposes action with intent, supporting evidence, specific owner, resources/coordination, assumptions, risk, desired indicator and anticipated consequence; competing teams may challenge. Director weighs evidence, uses advisory AI if enabled, decides final outcome and publishes only authorized consequences. Generate at least two plausible branching consequences per turn (timely accurate decision vs weak/unsupported decision), including one de-escalation possibility. No automatic legal or certification determination, and do not let AI self-execute privileged changes.

METRICS (EXERCISE ONLY): Model incident containment, evidence integrity and operational continuity as fictional 0–100 operational measures, plus contractual exposure and decision confidence as descriptive game states. State explicitly that these are not compliance scores, CMMC results or factual government status.

SCORING / LEARNING OBJECTIVES:
Score evidence-backed professional decision-making using (1) legal and contract interpretation 30%, (2) evidence validity 25%, (3) risk reduction 20%, (4) business judgment 15%, and (5) coordination 10%. Scores are entered or approved by the instructor, not invented as regulatory compliance results. Learning objectives: distinguish law vs contract vs standard/framework; identify applicable scope; assess third-party risk; verify audit evidence; build actionable POA&Ms; understand CMMC readiness restrictions; communicate defensible board recommendations.

REALISM AND SAFETY:
Use only fabricated company names, sample documents, synthetic logs and instructor-provided excerpts. Do not invent definitive regulatory citations, legal reporting deadlines, CMMC conditional status, verified exfiltration, or contract eligibility. References to NIST SP 800-37, NIST SP 800-53 CA-5, NIST SP 800-171, NIST CSF, CMMC, GDPR, and Florida law must be carefully qualified as potentially applicable based on scope and current authority. The Director must validate the current legal/contractual obligations before the session. No actual aircraft operations, classified information, real CUI, real keys, student private data or operational targeting instructions.
